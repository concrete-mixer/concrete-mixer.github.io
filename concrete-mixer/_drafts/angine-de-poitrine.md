Angine de Poitrine first transcended their native Quebec in February 2026 when their Seattle Radio KEXP session went viral. As my fingers aren't really on the pulse of contemporary music I didn't hear about them until two months later via the bass-playing YouTuber Davie504. (I had a near miss when I saw a headline about them on the Guardian, but the Guardian hypes so many things to excess I'm afraid I paid them no attention.)

Anyway, I beheld Khn and Klek de Poitrine's papier-mache costumes and instantly fell in love - as have a good many others. Much has been made of Khn's microtonal bass and electric guitars, the group's mathrocky time signatures, the use of loop pedals, Klek's immaculate drumming, and of course the duo's anonymity behind their spotty costumes. Angine de Poitrine's genius lies in all of these things, of course, but I think there's more to be teased out. Some have pointed to the fact that AI could never replicate Angine de Poitrine because they're just too idiosyncratic. (Sadly I think if you have enough source material to train on (Frank Zappa, Primus, etc) you can make AI replicate anything.) Perhaps that's true, but I think it says more about listeners' anxieties about the death of music than it does about AdP.

If AdP isn't an antidote to AI, what _can_ we say about them? Well, I like that when you see Angine de Poitrine perform you see everything about them. I'd be shocked if their two albums had been recorded with any overdubs; it's not that they couldn't if there was some effect they wanted that they'd never been able to achieve playing live, but having invested so much in having such a huge live sound for two musicians, not recording their songs as they play them live feels wrong.

Another aspect I enjoy is that AdP is that their music shifts effortlessly from prog to funk to jazz and back. There's a helping of north African psychedelia and there's also a bit surf rock. Although the music isn't particularly motorik there's something of the spirit of Neu!, and also of the Ramones. Maybe a bit of P-funk too, though in a very different way... The music has odd time signatures but you can bop to it without feeling like the band thinks it's smarter than you. In terms of performance, it's immediate: no one's hiding behind a bank of synthesisers or laptops. Admittedly Khn's bank of foot pedals (such elegant footwork manipulating them!) that serves a similar function, but that has an elemnt of performance virtuosity, so it's also a plus.

It's also about their "commitment to the bit". Not just their anonymity, but the fact that despite going viral they've continued to play the gigs they were already booked to do rather than exploit their success by switching to bigger venues. They know it probably wouldn't work - the magic is in being close to them, not miles away. If they want to tackle larger arenas, they're going to have to come up with a plan.

They look like dorks - esp poor old Khn in his atrocious cylindrical head - looks so uncomfortable on his shoulders - but somehow it's cool too. Then there's their cheerfully priapic noses. There's a slight medieval mummers meets masque ball vibe to Klek's headgear.

Then there's the alien personas. This performance on a Quebec TV show has a sort of muppet-like innocence. It's slightly bewildering connecting that back to the music.

It's been a long time since I've been so excited about something musical that's contemporary. Aespa's Supernova and Psy's Gangnam Style were the most recent examples, and I can say those songs feel a good deal more alien to me than Angine de Poitrine. I love that they're never going to wank on stage about political causes, or dedicate a song to a girlfriend. I love that they only talk gibberish - not accidentally like other bands, but intentionally. 

What next for AdP? Well, they'll ride the excitement for the rest of this year with what they've got, but they'll get bored and want to change things up. There's so much potential, and not just musically: a sitcom perhaps, or a movie directed by Michel Gondry. Lunchbox stickers for kids; an endorsement for fishfingers. A performance at Giza seems apt, if the Egyptian authorities aren't too suspicious. There's also a pyramid at Glastonbury...
