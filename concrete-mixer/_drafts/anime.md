I'm always a bit mistrustful of anime. It can be a bit pervy and weird (even, and perhaps especially, the good ones), and if you're a teen consumer there's a danger you'll turn be a greasy lad with a collection of bikini-clad dolls, or a "girl of size" perplexingly dressed like Alice in Wonderland.

I've trodden gingerly, but have surveyed a fair bit, and here is my report.

# Miyazaki

He's pretty good! It's hard to pick a favourite. Nausicaa of the Valley of the Wind has had the biggest effect on me. The animation isn't the best, but I found the scope of the story so compelling I went out and bought the manga. Princess Mononoke is another highlight, along with Totoro and Spirited Away. The Boy and the Heron too. You can't go wrong with any of them, really.

I'll add that I share Miyazaki's pessimism (or realism) about humanity.

# Berzerk

I'd been aware of Berserk through exposure to Susumu Hirasawa's superb soundtrack to the anime, but what I'd seen of Berserk - mediaeval fantasy helmed by a bloke with an unfeasibly large sword. (What I didn't know then is when you're set against absurdly over-powered enemies of cosmic proportions, an unfeasibly large sword is the least you need.)

In 2016 I began paying more attention. What I found was a great detail darker and scarier than I'd expected, and to this day I've neither read the manga or watched any of the anime adaptations in full. To be frank, I kind of fear Berzerk. And yet, for a while I found it darkly beguiling.




It's a one-armed swordsman story, set in a mediaeval fantasy world only it exists in a world where not only are ghosts and ghouls real, God is out to get you.

Berzerk a very brutal tale of betrayal and revenge set against a background of Lovecraftian cosmic horror. The best part is the Golden Age arc where swordsman Guts (terrible name, but partly based on a mediaeval mercenary called Gotz) is betrayed by his best mate Griffith and his mates end up sacrificed to a ravening horde of demons during a rite called the Eclipse.

The Eclipse is one of the most stark and brutal depictions of horror and violence I've encountered, and it's always sobering to realise that atrocities remain very much a part of human behaviour.

After the Eclipse a maimed but prodigiously strong Guts goes off seeking revenge on Griffiths and demons who killed his crew. The arcs that follow aren't as compelling; indeed there are points when I feel I'm following a D&D campaign.

Berzerk has run since 1990; unfortunately its creator ran out of steam in the 2000s and stopped publishing as frequently, making his artwork more ornate, and also going on hiatus to play video games. Or so I have read. Rather sadly he tied in 2021, with Berzerk unfinished. His assistants are continuing the story without him, which is their right, given their choice doing that or being unemployed. I expect purists won't be impressed.

Perhaps the best part of Berzerk (and what alerted me to its existence) is Susumu Hirasawa's score for various Berzerk anime series. If you want to listen to something stirring and martial, he's your man.
